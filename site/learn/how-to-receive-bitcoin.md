To receive bitcoin, you need a Bitcoin address.

A wallet you control can generate one. Or an exchange can assign one and receive the bitcoin on your behalf.

Think of it this way:

- Your physical wallet holds your cash.
- Your bank holds money in your bank account.

Likewise:

- Your Bitcoin wallet controls your bitcoin.
- A crypto exchange holds bitcoin in your exchange account.

## What is a Bitcoin address?

A Bitcoin address tells the network where to send bitcoin. It looks something like this:

`bc1qxy2kgdygjrsqtzq2n0yrf2493p83kkfjhx0wlh`

You don't need to memorize or type it manually. Just copy and paste it, or let the sender scan its QR code.

A Bitcoin address is meant to be shared. It gives others a way to send bitcoin to your wallet.

## The part you do not share

Many wallets give you a seed phrase: usually 12 or 24 ordinary words that serve as the wallet's backup. Anyone with those words can restore the wallet and spend your bitcoin.

Your address and seed phrase play contrasting roles:

- Share the address. It's how you get bitcoin.
- Protect the seed phrase. It's how you keep control of your bitcoin.

## How to get a Bitcoin address

We favor self-custody, so we recommend a wallet you control. If you don't want to compare wallets, pick one of these:

- [Blockstream App](https://blockstream.com/app/) — iOS, Android, and Windows
- [BlueWallet](https://bluewallet.io/) — iOS and Android
- [Wasabi Wallet](https://wasabiwallet.io/) — Windows

Once you've installed one:

1. **Create a wallet.** Look for **Get Started**, **+**, or **Add Wallet**, then create a new Bitcoin wallet.
2. **Follow the prompts to back up your wallet.**
3. **Open Receive.** Your wallet may ask for a label before showing your address and QR code.
4. **Share it.** Send the address or show the QR code.

If you use an exchange, sign in and look for **Deposit**, or search its help section for “deposit bitcoin.”

This article covers payments sent to Bitcoin addresses. The Lightning Network can make fast, small payments more practical, but receiving over Lightning works differently and deserves its own article.

## How to share the address

If the sender is with you, you can let them scan the QR code from your Receive screen.

Otherwise, copy and paste the address into a message. Avoid typing it by hand. Before sending, compare the first and last few characters with the address in your wallet.

It's good practice to get a fresh address for each payment. Get one by going through **Receive** again.

## A few practical questions

**How do I know I've been paid?** The transaction will usually appear in your wallet as pending or unconfirmed. It becomes confirmed once a miner includes it in a block. One confirmation may be enough for an ordinary low-risk payment; larger or higher-risk transactions may justify more. [Learn more about pending and confirmed Bitcoin payments.](/learn/bitcoin-pending-vs-confirmed-payments/)

**Can someone take my bitcoin if I share an address?** No. An address lets someone send bitcoin to you, not spend yours. Its activity is public, which is why fresh addresses are better for privacy.

**Do I pay a fee to receive bitcoin?** Usually not. The sender pays the network fee. An exchange may charge its own, but Bitcoin doesn't. If someone asks you to pay before you can receive bitcoin, that's a scam.

**What if the amount is wrong?** If it's short, ask the sender for the rest. If it's over, ask whether it was intentional. If not, refund the difference to an address they give you.
